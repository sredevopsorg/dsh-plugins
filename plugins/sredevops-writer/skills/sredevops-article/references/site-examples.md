# Site examples

These are calibration references, not templates. Open one or two when you need to hear the register. Study the rhythm, the opening move, and the way technical detail sits next to the joke. Do not copy sentences, headings, or paragraph order.

## Spanish

### News and analysis: Distro Zero and the distroless illusion

https://www.sredevops.org/es/distro-zero-y-el-fin-de-la-ilusion-distroless-como-traefik-redibuja-la-seguridad-en-gateways/

- Opens with a strong claim about an industry belief, then immediately complicates it.
- Uses concrete inventory (what is inside the image) instead of abstract security language.
- Mixes a table, a shell transcript, and a diagram without losing the argument.
- The references section is part of the reporting, not an afterthought.

### Opinion and experience: on-call myths and creeds

https://www.sredevops.org/es/los-mitos-y-credos-respecto-a-turnos-o-estar-de-guardia/

- The source article uses first-person experience; do not imitate that by inventing incidents. Every claim is still actionable.
- Short H3 sections, each one a rule the reader can adopt.
- The humor is dry and situational, not a quota of jokes per paragraph.

### Community and event writing: DevOpsDays Chile

https://www.sredevops.org/es/devopsdays-llega-a-chile-acaso-no-eramos-dignos/

- The playful register is stronger here because the piece is a community invitation, not a security advisory.
- Local references land because the audience shares them.
- Notice how quickly it returns to practical details: date, venue, audience, call for proposals.

## English

### Tutorial: Freelens

https://www.sredevops.org/en/freelens-taking-back-control-of-your-kubernetes-clusters-with-a-truly-open-source-desktop-client/

- Opens with a familiar operator pain, not a product description.
- Installation paths are separated by platform, with commands that match the platform.
- The article earns the right to recommend the tool by showing the work.

### Security opinion: sudo, chroot, and nsswitch

https://www.sredevops.org/en/sudos-latest-trick-when-chroot-and-nsswitch-conspire-against-you-cve-2025-32462/

- The sarcasm is aimed at the situation and the trust model, not at the reader.
- Technical specifics (CVE, versions, mechanism) are never sacrificed for the bit.
- The piece makes a broader point about privilege and assumptions without leaving the vulnerability behind.

### Explainer: what is WebAssembly

https://www.sredevops.org/en/what-is-web-assembly-wasm-and-what-is-it-for/

- Defines a moving target without writing a textbook chapter.
- Uses comparisons the audience already understands, then shows where the comparison breaks.
- Ends with the practical question: what would you actually use this for?

## What to borrow

- The opening move: a concrete tension, not a definition.
- The confidence to state a judgment and then show the evidence.
- The willingness to explain the mechanism instead of name-dropping it.
- The references section as part of the article's credibility.

## What not to borrow

- Specific jokes or metaphors.
- The exact section order.
- The article's length. Match the assignment, not the example.
- The local references unless the new piece is also about that community or event.
